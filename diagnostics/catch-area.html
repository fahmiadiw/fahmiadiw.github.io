Key findings

Coverage looks complete on paper, but one in five residents is far from referral care.

Every one of the 9 sub-districts has a puskesmas and 89.9% of residents live within 5 km of one. Yet all 5 general hospitals sit in Pringsewu sub-district, leaving 91,670 people (20.3%) beyond 10 km and 27,231 beyond 15 km.

The gap is concentrated, not spread evenly.

Three sub-districts (Adi Luwih, Pardasuka, Pagelaran Utara) hold 21% of the population but 88% of residents beyond 10 km of a hospital. District averages (6.8 km) dilute this almost completely.

Adding workload changes the priority list.

Ranked on distance alone, Pardasuka comes first. Adding people per doctor moves Bandung Baru to #1 (far, outpatient only, 44,522 per doctor) and pulls Rejosari from #7 to #4: it sits in town with no distance problem but carries 43,830 people per doctor. Adiluwih drops (#4 → #6) because its inpatient, PONED-capable puskesmas is well staffed. Rejosari needs staff, not a new building.

I build these as reproducible spatial-tabular workflows (Python, PostGIS, QGIS and WebGIS), so a program team can re-run the access check whenever a facility opens, a boundary changes or new staffing and service data arrive. Village and catchment keys use official region codes, so the output joins cleanly to DHIS2-style org-unit hierarchies. Data. Village population: Disdukcapil Pringsewu (PRIMA portal). 21 facilities: coordinates, inpatient/PONED status and staffing collected and verified manually; 4 points corrected by point-in-polygon check. Boundaries: BPS 2020 via HDX.
Method. Straight-line distance from village centroid to nearest facility (UTM 48S). Catchments = nearest-puskesmas model, not official Dinkes work areas. Thresholds: 5 km (primary care), 10 km (referral).
Limits. Distance is not travel time (next version: OSM road network). Doctor counts per puskesmas are point-in-time.
